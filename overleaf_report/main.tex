\begin{abstract}
Wearable human activity recognition (HAR) faces missing, noisy, biased, and frozen sensor streams while continuous multi-device sensing incurs acquisition and communication costs. We implement per-IMU classifiers, observable quality indicators, reliability-weighted probability fusion, and a controller that selects the next sensing window's active IMUs. Eight-subject leave-one-subject-out evaluation on official PAMAP2 six-class data gave adaptive random-forest clean observed-label macro-F1 $0.8574\pm0.0861$ at normalized sensing-cost proxy 0.5281. All-sensor uniform fusion gave $0.8891\pm0.0726$ at cost 1.0; a fixed wrist-plus-chest pair gave $0.9150\pm0.0482$ at cost 0.667. Under moderate wrist dropout, adaptive macro-F1 was 0.7919 versus 0.8501 for all-sensor uniform fusion. A subsequent inner-validation search selected a larger forest in six folds and changed the policy in three, but its held-out fixed-six-class clean macro-F1 was 0.8227 versus 0.8428 for the original configuration replayed under the same metric. The original policy reduced the simulated acquisition/communication cost proxy by 47.2\%; neither configuration improved recognition over the best of the fixed comparators. Hardware energy and clinical performance are both unmeasured.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
The Internet of Things interconnects sensors, gateways, and applications. IoT with AI enables in-situ inference to convert measurements into decisions. Body-worn sensors enable activity tracking continuously, but realistic streams are missing packets, have sensor breakdown, and encounter noise. Reliability-aware fusion is able to safeguard decisions after sensing has been done, it does not reverse the cost of sensing. The research question is, can observable sensor health, HAR uncertainty, and an explicit sensing budget drive the configuration used in the \emph{next} window. This implementation provides a reproducible controller, and evaluation protocol, not a new fusion algorithm or measured hardware energy advantage.
In the target deployment 
(Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}), each IMU collects a set of local motion channels and transmits a window or features to a local gateway. The gateway executes a small activity classifier, merges the sensor predictions that it can, and issues a control command to another window start. A cloud service might ingest a portion of these summaries for long-term monitoring, but the experiment does not instantiate a cloud backend or a physical wireless protocol. The acquisition ready command can be the architectural feature that alters the volume of future sampling and transmission. Probability fusion simply operates post-acquisition and does not have a similar effect.

The fault model is significant because high activity recognition accuracy on clean prerecorded data only provides weak evidence for an operational node. The wrist IMU can go missing, the wireless interval can disappear, or the channel can be frozen or drifting with good numbers still flowing in. Applying a uniform burden of trust, a wrongly shifted value could be prioritized over a failed device. If all three devices are permanently kept active, there would be no chance to reduce the measurement set, but instead favor activity recognition (as we do in wrist-centric), without regard for sensing-cost.

Our aims are to (i) map existing PAMAP2 channels to three physical IMUs, (ii) build sensor-specific HAR probability models with training-only scaling and held-out subjects, (iii) estimate reliability without the injected-fault label at inference, (iv) fuse only predictions from presently active IMUs, and (v) select an inexpensive, robust next-window configuration. We compare this combination against: anchor-only, fixed-pair, all-sensor uniform, and all-sensor reliability-weighted variants. These are implementations in a common simulation, not de novo reproduction of the surveyed systems.

Our contribution is deliberately small. We evaluate a narrowly-tailored, straightforward rule-based controller that avoids the drawbacks of training on novel subjects, and whose decisions can be carefully examined. A more nuanced controller might generate a better trade-off. In our experiments we therefore seek to determine the value of this new-robustness countermeasure, and to identify conditions where it breaks down. A negative result helps establish whether our all-sensor approach is more effective at handling faults, in which case resource, budget, or policy considerations are better re-engineered rather than excused as a measure of resilience.

\section{Literature Survey and Gap}
Centaur uses denoising and multimodal fusion for incomplete HAR signals \cite{centaur}. Dong \emph{et al.} evaluate wearable sensor reliability through multiple criteria and evidential fusion \cite{dong}. Zuo \emph{et al.} combine sensor-level probabilities with self-reliability, discrepancy, and uncertainty \cite{zuo}. These approaches operate on acquired readings. AdaSense changes a wearable sensing configuration using an accuracy and power trade-off \cite{adasense}. Demirel et al. have addressed dynamic ECG sensor selection using a contextual bandit \cite{demirel}. CoSS performs sensor and sampling rate selection via trainable scores and pruning \cite{coss}. The difference identified in our sample of six papers from Part 1 of this review is a combination of online signal-quality gating, predictive uncertainty, and a next-window sensing budget for injected fault scenarios. This is a specific comparison and not a claim of precedence for that relevant body of literature.

The denoising autoencoder and the attention-enhanced multimodal network used by Centaur to handle noisy and missing streams of HAR data \cite{centaur}. Its strength is learning a reconstruction and recognition procedure across all the sensor channels. Its downside for our application is timing -- the corrupted measurements are already received before they are cleaned. Our system does not mimic Centaur's network, instead, we decide if an observable quality score can influence the next channel to acquire. Dong \emph{et al.} merge conflict and imprecision in a body sensor network reliability analysis \cite{dong}. While it may indicate that weak sensor evidence should be disregarded, the evidence combination method is not the same as our normalized probability average approach. As our implementation uses physically intelligible signal indicators, a dropout introduced in our process can be detected without consulting the test label.

Zuo \emph{et al.} evaluate the sensor's decision via self-reliability, discrepancy and uncertainty prior to the evidential fusion of their sensor streams \cite{zuo}. Despite demonstrating the value of sensor-specific decision streams, the reliability this approach yields is both validation-driven behaviour and a form of disagreement at the probability level. Our controller uses a less broad-based assessment of signal quality over time and takes the fused entropy into account when deciding in the next window. There is a marked difference: a prediction can be uncertain if two functioning sensors disagree, yet a flatline can be premature. Neither piece of information should be thought of as a complete replacement for the other.

Ada Sense demonstrates that activity-aware sensing setups can result in an increase in the power consumed on its wearable device \cite{adasense} and provides an example of pre-acquisition adaptation along with a stable change in configuration. While the focus of the Ada Sense system is on changing the wearable classifier's sensor mode, we introduce degradation of IMUs and use an estimated health gate in our experiment. Demirel \emph{et al.} select the ECG leads online with a neural contextual bandit \cite{demirel}. This is a promising approach to on-line resource management, but the cardiac task, lead geometry, and reward function do not map directly onto three IMUs, motion sensors. Our na{\"i}ve policy does not need to learn a reward from test subjects, using manually selected thresholds instead.

CoSS jointly optimizes sensor modality and sampling rate with trainable scores followed by pruning \cite {coss}. It addresses data efficiency and model footprint, but its selected configuration is a product of training rather than a direct reaction to a transient stuck or missing sensor during a test stream. Together, the six studies separate three useful ideas: robust processing after data arrive, decision-level source discounting, and resource-aware sensing before acquisition. Our limited gap is their conjunction in one auditable fault-injection benchmark. Comparing their published metrics numerically with ours would be misleading because activities, subject splits, modalities, fault processes, and cost units differ.

\section{Dataset and Preprocessing}
PAMAP2 contains nine participants, 18 activity labels, three 100-Hz IMUs on the dominant wrist/hand, chest, and ankle, and a lower-rate heart-rate monitor \cite{pamap2}. The 54-column protocol files contain timestamp, activity ID, heart rate, and 17 values per IMU. This implementation selects the $\pm16g$ accelerometer and gyroscope axes from each IMU: zero-based columns 4--6 and 10--12 for hand, 21--23 and 27--29 for chest, and 38--40 and 44--46 for ankle. "wrist" node's six selected channels of complete hand IMU (not just accelerometer). Labels 1--6 for a six-class feasibility study (none of activity 0 and other classes). Choose due to miniaturization and to defer other classsm and a PAMAP2 comparison.

Per-subject windows have 256 samples (2.56 s) and stride 128 (50\% overlap). A window is retained when at least 80\% of samples have the majority label, and windows spanning timestamp gaps after filtering are rejected. Channel means and standard deviations are fit on training subjects only. The held-out subject is never used for fitting. When bounded interpolation is applied, zero is assigned to long natural gaps; since this approach can mask the presence of genuine missing values, fault detection results are based only on the injected faults until the raw missing masks are kept.

\section{System Methodology}
At window $t$, active IMUs produce signals $x_i(t)$. A sensor-specific random forest, or optional compact CNN--LSTM, yields a class probability vector $p_i(t)$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:fusion}). The baseline estimator combines missingness, flatline, first-difference noise, and distribution-shift penalties $q_{ij}(t)\in[0,1]$:
\begin{equation}
r_i(t)=\operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}\left(1-\sum_j\alpha_jq_{ij}(t)\right).
\end{equation}
Weights $\alpha_j$ are normalized and set in the configuration file. A training-calibrated distribution-shift indicator is included as a bias/drift proxy. Its threshold has not yet been validated on official-data fault streams. The normalized weighted fusion is
\begin{equation}
p(k\mid t)=\frac{\sum_{i\in A_t}r_i(t)p_i(k\mid t)}{\sum_{i\in A_t}r_i(t)}.
\end{equation}
When the active total weight falls below 0.05, the implementation uses a uniform \emph{class prior} rather than trusting any sensor prediction; this produces high entropy and can prompt a new sensing configuration. Uncertainty is normalized entropy $u(t)=-\sum_{k=1}^Kp(k\mid t)\log p(k\mid t)/\log K$. The equal-weight acquisition and communication \emph{proxy} is $C(A_t)=\sum_{i\in A_t}c_i/\sum_i c_i$. It is neither joules nor measured battery power.

The controller observes only active IMUs at $t$, then chooses $A_{t+1}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:loop}). High entropy or low active-sensor reliability may activate an affordable optional IMU. Low entropy or detected failure may deactivate an optional IMU after a dwell period. The wrist anchor stays active. Inactive reliability is retained from its last observation, discounted by $0.98$ per unobserved window for candidate ranking, and refreshed by periodic probes; probing contributes to the cost proxy. A configured budget bounds the next active set. The evaluation must log masks, uncertainty, reliability, actions, and predictions per window before claiming policy behavior.

\section{Implementation Details}
Three separate random forests provide subset-compatible predictions, one for each selected six-axis IMU. Each 256-sample window is summarized by 72 time-domain features: nine statistics for each of six channels and two vector magnitudes. The reduced-compute runs use 50 trees per IMU, depth at most 15, minimum leaf size two, balanced class weights, and seed 42. A comparison family uses two temporal convolutions (32 and 64 filters, kernel widths seven and five), max pooling, a unidirectional 64-unit LSTM, 0.3 dropout, and a six-class output. The reduced mode trains for up to five epochs with Adam at learning rate 0.001, batch size 64, and validation-based early stopping. Separate models mean an inactive IMU is not replaced by an invented current signal.

The weights assigned to the quality scores are 0.30 for missingness, 0.25 for flatline, 0.25 for first difference noise, and 0.20 for training-calibrated distribution shift; degradation is initiated by the controller when the reading falls below $r{\rm low}=0.40$. The entropy thresholds are $u{\rm high}=0.65$ and $u_{\rm low}=0.35$. All of the IMUs have the same proxy weight and the total budget is 0.667, which enables the use of the wrist together with one optional IMU. The minimum dwell time is four windows, and an IMU that is inactive is included in the probe process after 20 windows of non-observation. These are preliminary engineering values and not validated thresholds.

Faults are added by the test harness after the training-only scaling has been applied, to the held out stream. The missing sections are indicated by NaN values, which the reliability estimator can detect, and are changed to zero only in those classifiers that make use of finite features. The amount of Gaussian noise and drift are therefore given in standardised channel units, not in physical acceleration or angular-rate units. The six configurations are dropout, intermittent blocks, noise, drift, flatline and noise plus missingness. The same corrupted arrays are used by competing methods within a scenario. Offline batch caching accelerates scenario replay, but cached predictions from an inactive IMU are never passed to the controller or fusion. Such replay timing cannot be interpreted as deployment inference latency.

\section{Experimental Protocol}
The runner compares wrist only, wrist plus chest, all-sensor uniform fusion, all-sensor reliability-weighted fusion, and adaptive reliability-weighted fusion with shared per-sensor models. The reduced-compute full-fast mode performs leave-one-subject-out evaluation across the eight subjects with six-class data. Each fold uses the other subjects for fitting and a separate subject for CNN validation. The quick mode uses two development subjects and one held-out subject. Faults are injected only into held-out windows: complete or partial dropout, intermittent blocks, Gaussian noise, flatline, drift, and combined corruption. A standalone ablation replays a saved fold with one policy component changed at a time. Runs save input hashes and clean and moderate-dropout adaptive window logs. Report accuracy, macro precision, macro recall, macro F1, confusion matrices, fault-detection AUROC, active-node ratio, proxy cost, switches, and host latency from actual logs. Relative proxy reduction is $100(1-C_{\rm method}/C_{\rm all})\%$; macro-F1 degradation is $100(F1_{\rm clean}-F1_{\rm fault})$ percentage points.

A follow-up nested search reused each outer fold's training-only scaler and saved 50-tree random forests, and trained a 100-tree, depth-20, minimum-leaf-two candidate on that fold's six fitting subjects. Its seventh non-test subject selected the model using all-uniform fixed-six-class macro-F1 weighted across clean, moderate dropout, noise, and combined corruption (0.35, 0.25, 0.20, 0.20). The same validation subject selected among the original policy; a conservative policy with entropy thresholds 0.75/0.45, dwell 8, probe interval 40; and an early-reinforcement policy with thresholds 0.50/0.25, dwell 8, probe interval 12. The policy objective subtracted 0.10 times clean normalized cost and 0.02 times clean switches per window from that recognition score. Wrist anchor and 0.667 budget set. Selection never read its own outer test subject. The resulting choices were benchmarked across six fault types at three severity levels with seed 42 and deterministic per-subject fault streams. These subjects had been studied in the original run, making this an exploratory nested followup rather than an external validation cohort.

\subsection{Reproducibility and Boundary of Measurements}

The nested run records a clean test and 18 faulted tests for each held-out fold: the 6 fault settings at mild, moderate, and severe. Its protocol records the model candidates, policy thresholds, objective weights, and random seed. The saved per-fold records track the selected model and policy, validation scores, test metrics, and cost traces; clean and moderate-dropout adaptive logs track the predictions and active masks window-by-window. F1 can be recomputed from those predictions and toggles from neighboring masks, on whether to check the aggregate independently of its CSV dump. The result figures are assembled from the saved records, not from the rounded values in the manuscript.

The $C(A_t)$ cost simply counts active IMUs, weighing equally. It does not include sensor warm-up, radio retransmissions, computation, battery chemistry, or the cost of a control message; thus, the presence of a mask toggle is a stability indicator, not a measured switching-energy penalty. Similarly, fault-detection AUROC measures the injected labels it receives from the simulator; thresholded detection F1 shows that obtaining a high-ranking score does not necessarily imply reliable alarms at the specified threshold. Host-side replay time incorporates caching and batching; it does not quantify latency over a wearable gateway. All these boundaries must be considered when viewing the $47.2\%$ proxy reduction: It is a synthetic characteristic of the recorded masks, rather than the physical energy savings that are yet to be measured.

\section{Limitations and Conclusion}
This implementation is a simulation over prerecorded IMU streams; physical node scheduling, radio energy, and clinical generalization are not measured. The eight-subject historical evaluation demonstrates a 47.2\% reduction in a normalized sensing-cost proxy for lower clean and faulted recognition than the strongest fixed comparator. The nested search failed to improve the held-out recognition--cost trade-off, and illustrates unstable transfer from one validation person to the next. Reliability weighting is more helpful under some injected analog faults than under explicit missingness. Thresholds and cost weights require calibration that is informed by the hardware; the switching overhead may partially cancel this benefit. Future work should test calibration, natural failures, more robust multi-subject inner validation, all available activity classes, use of richer uncertainty control, and more subjects and devices. The required institutional similarity report and group presentation are each separate submission steps.
